# Evidence base

The principles every plan in this repo follows. Each one names its source so you can check it. Where the
evidence is mixed, it says so.

---

## 1. Safety screening before starting
- **PAR-Q+ screening.** Apparently healthy adults can start moderate exercise without medical clearance.
  Anyone with known heart, metabolic or kidney disease, or with symptoms, should get clearance first.
  *Source: ACSM pre-participation screening algorithm (Riebe et al., MSSE 2015); PAR-Q+ (Warburton et al., 2011).*

## 2. How much activity, minimum
- **Adults:** 150–300 min/week moderate aerobic activity (or 75–150 vigorous), plus muscle-strengthening work
  for all major muscle groups on **≥2 days/week**. Less sitting helps whatever the dose.
  *Source: WHO Guidelines on Physical Activity and Sedentary Behaviour (Bull et al., BJSM 2020).*

## 3. Resistance training: building muscle and strength
| Variable | What the research supports | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Weekly volume** | About 10+ hard sets per muscle per week gives more growth than fewer. Gains keep rising up to ~20 sets but flatten out. Beginners grow well on less. | Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger, *J Sports Sci* 2017; Pelland et al. 2024 meta-regression |
| **Frequency** | Train each muscle **≥2×/week** when volume is equal. Splitting the volume over more days matters less than total volume. | Schoenfeld et al., *Sports Med* 2016 & 2019 |
| **Load / reps** | Muscle grows about equally across **~6–30 reps** if sets go near failure. Heavier loads (≤6RM) build more **strength**. | Schoenfeld et al., *JSCR* 2017; Lopez et al., *MSSE* 2021 |
| **Effort (proximity to failure)** | Stop sets about **0–3 reps short of failure** (RIR 0–3). Going to full failure all the time adds fatigue and little extra muscle. | Refalo et al., *Sports Med* 2023; Robinson et al. 2024 |
| **Rest between sets** | **≥2 min** on big lifts gives better results than very short rests. | Schoenfeld et al., *JSCR* 2016; Singer et al. 2024 |
| **Progression** | Add load or reps over time (double progression). Track every session. | ACSM progression position stand, *MSSE* 2009 |
| **Range of motion** | Full ROM, or a bias toward the lengthened position, is as good as or better than partial ROM. | Pallarés et al. 2021; Wolf et al. 2023 |
| **Deloads** | Lower volume or effort every 4–8 weeks, or when performance drops. Common practice; direct evidence is limited. | Bell et al., *Sports Med – Open* 2023 (expert consensus) |

## 4. Cardio / conditioning
- **Zone 2 / moderate steady state** improves aerobic base and health markers. **HIIT** reaches similar VO₂max
  gains in less time. A mix works well.
  *Source: Milanović et al., *Sports Med* 2015 (HIIT vs MICT meta-analysis).*
- **Concurrent training** (cardio + lifting) slightly reduces strength/power gains, mostly when cardio volume
  is high or running-based right before lifting. It does not meaningfully reduce muscle growth.
  *Source: Schumann et al., *Sports Med* 2022.*
- **VO₂max and fitness** strongly predict lower all-cause mortality.
  *Source: Mandsager et al., *JAMA Netw Open* 2018.*

## 5. Nutrition
### Energy balance (fat loss / gain)
- Body weight change is driven by energy balance. Diet type (low-carb vs low-fat) makes no meaningful
  difference to fat loss **when calories and protein are equal**; adherence is what matters.
  *Source: Hall & Guo, *Gastroenterology* 2017; DIETFITS trial (Gardner et al., *JAMA* 2018).*
- **Fat loss pace:** ~0.5–1.0% body weight/week keeps more muscle than faster loss.
  *Source: Helms, Aragon & Fitschen, *JISSN* 2014; Garthe et al., *IJSNEM* 2011.*
- **Muscle gain:** a small surplus (~+5–10% kcal, or ~0.25–0.5% body weight/month gain for trained people,
  more for beginners) limits fat gain. *Source: Iraki et al., *Sports* 2019.*
- **Calorie starting point:** estimate with Mifflin-St Jeor × activity factor. Then correct it from your
  real 2–3-week weight trend; formulas can be off by ±10–15%.

### Protein
- **1.6 g/kg/day** is where extra protein stops adding muscle gain on average; up to **2.2 g/kg** covers
  nearly everyone. *Source: Morton et al., *BJSM* 2018 meta-analysis.*
- During a diet, **~1.8–2.7 g/kg of lean mass** (or 2.0–2.4 g/kg body weight) helps keep muscle.
  *Source: Helms et al., *IJSNEM* 2014; ISSN position stand (Jäger et al., *JISSN* 2017).*
- Spread across **3–5 meals of ~0.3–0.4 g/kg** each. *Source: Schoenfeld & Aragon, *JISSN* 2018.*

### Carbs, fat, fibre
- Fat: ≥ ~20–25% of calories (hormonal health). Carbs: fill the rest; more carbs help high training volumes.
  *Source: ACSM/AND/DC joint position stand on nutrition & athletic performance (Thomas et al., 2016).*
- Fibre: ~25–38 g/day; whole-food, minimally processed diet. Ultra-processed diets made people eat ~500 kcal/day
  more in a controlled trial. *Source: Hall et al., *Cell Metab* 2019; Reynolds et al., *Lancet* 2019.*

### Supplements (only well-supported ones)
| Supplement | Evidence | Dose |
|---|---|---|
| **Creatine monohydrate** | Strong: improves strength, muscle and training capacity; safe in healthy people | 3–5 g/day, any time |
| **Caffeine** | Strong: improves performance | 3–6 mg/kg ~60 min before training |
| **Protein powder** | Convenience only: same as food protein | As needed to hit target |
| **Vitamin D** | Only if deficient (blood test) | Per doctor |

*Sources: ISSN creatine position stand (Kreider et al., 2017); ISSN caffeine position stand (Guest et al., 2021);
IOC consensus statement on supplements (Maughan et al., *BJSM* 2018).* Most other products have weak or no evidence.

## 6. Recovery
- **Sleep:** 7–9 h. Sleep restriction reduces muscle protein synthesis and makes dieting cost more muscle.
  *Source: Nedeltcheva et al., *Ann Intern Med* 2010; Lamon et al., *Physiol Rep* 2021.*
- **Daily steps / NEAT** make up a large and variable part of energy use; tracking steps helps fat loss.
  ~7,000–10,000 steps/day is linked with lower mortality. *Source: Paluch et al., *Lancet Public Health* 2022.*

## 7. Tracking and adherence
- Self-monitoring (weighing, food logging, training logs) is one of the most consistent predictors of success.
  *Source: Burke et al., *JADA* 2011 systematic review.*
- Use the **weekly average** body weight; daily weight swings 1–2 kg from water and gut content.

## 8. What this coaching will NOT do
- Spot reduction ("burn belly fat with crunches"): does not work (Ramírez-Campillo et al., 2013).
- Detox, fat burners, "metabolic damage" claims, anabolic-window panic, or extreme diets.
- Diagnose injuries or medical conditions: that goes to a doctor or physiotherapist.

## 9. Football-specific
- **Nordic hamstring curls** roughly halve hamstring injuries (van der Horst et al., *AJSM* 2015; van Dyk et al.,
  *BJSM* 2019 meta-analysis). Low weekly volume is enough (Presland et al. 2018).
- **Copenhagen adductor programme** reduced groin problems ~41% (Harøy et al., *BJSM* 2019).
- **FIFA 11+** warm-up reduces injuries ~30% in amateur footballers (Sadigursky et al., *BMC Sports Sci Med Rehabil* 2017).
- **Balance/proprioceptive training** after an ankle sprain reduced recurrence ~35% (Hupperets et al., *BMJ* 2009).
- **Strength training** improves sprint and jump in footballers (Silva et al., *IJSPP* 2015); once a week
  maintains in-season gains (Rønnestad et al., *JSCR* 2011).
- **4×4 min HIIT at 90–95% HRmax** improved VO₂max, match distance and sprints (Helgerud et al., *MSSE* 2001).
- **Session-RPE** (Foster et al. 2001) and short wellness questionnaires (McLean et al. 2010) are validated, cheap
  ways to monitor load. The "acute:chronic ratio" as an injury predictor is disputed (Impellizzeri et al. 2020);
  we just avoid big week-to-week spikes.
- **Match nutrition:** periodized carbs (6–8 g/kg MD-1 and MD), 30–60 g/h during play, protein after
  (UEFA expert group statement, Collins et al., *BJSM* 2021).
- **Sleep extension** improved sprint times and shooting accuracy in athletes (Mah et al., *Sleep* 2011).
- **Cold-water immersion** after strength training blunts gains (Roberts et al., *J Physiol* 2015).
- **Alcohol** after exercise reduces muscle protein synthesis (Parr et al., *PLoS One* 2014).
- **Pre-sleep casein** (~40 g) increases overnight muscle protein synthesis (Snijders et al., *J Nutr* 2015; Res et al. 2012).
